Calculation methodology

How the repayment engine, equal-budget comparisons and cost measures work.

Repayment mortgage formula

For principal P, monthly rate r and n monthly payments, the level payment is P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^(−n)). The annual nominal percentage rate is divided by 1,200 to obtain r. At a zero rate the payment is P ÷ n.

Each month, interest is the opening balance multiplied by r. The payment reduces interest and principal, and is capped at the amount needed to repay the loan. Extra payments occur at month end; a lump sum occurs before month one. Payments stay unchanged after an overpayment, shortening the term.

Interest, cash and principal

Cash paid includes deposits, fees and repayments. Mortgage financing cost is interest plus fees and entered charges. Principal repayment is not treated as an expense because it reduces a debt. A financed product fee increases the starting loan and accrues interest; the fee itself is counted once in financing cost.

Two-year and five-year fixes

The comparison lasts 60 months. Option A recalculates its repayment at month 25 using the outstanding balance, remaining term and entered reset rate. One new fee is paid then in cash. Option B keeps its entered rate throughout. The equal-cost reset rate is found numerically between 0% and 20%; no threshold is reported outside that range.

Equal cash budgets

Overpayment, rent-versus-buy and moving comparisons give both paths equal starting cash and equal monthly contributions. Each path saves unused funds at the entered annual effective after-tax savings rate, compounded monthly. This includes payments freed when a mortgage is repaid. Net cost is starting resources plus shared cash contributions minus ending equity and savings.

Deposit and two-home purchase comparisons use cash paid minus equity gained, and subtract the savings interest earned on the difference in upfront cash. Monthly payment differences are not reinvested in those tools. This simpler convention is disclosed in their reports.

Property and moving

Property values compound by the annual scenario rate. Rent changes once a year. Monthly ownership or running costs stay level. Purchase tax is an entered amount; we store no SDLT, LBTT or LTT bands. Selling agent fees are a percentage of the current sale value and should include VAT.

Extension costs plus contingency are fully financed immediately. Added property value is independent of construction cost. Moving carries existing equity into the replacement home, pays costs in cash and invests any released equity. Finance rates and terms are shared between moving options; staged works, bridging loans and temporary financing are outside V1.

Break-even and thresholds

Mortgage deal break-even is the first month Option B has lower cumulative interest and fees than A. We flag later reversals. A balance threshold searches £1,000–£2,000,000 for equal period costs; if no crossing exists in that range, none is stated. No universal break-even is claimed for equity-based housing choices.

Precision and exclusions

Calculations use unrounded floating-point values, displaying pounds rounded to the nearest pound. Monthly interest is an approximation to lender schedules, which can use daily accrual and different rounding. There is no inflation discounting, automatic tax calculation, affordability assessment or forecast of future rates. Equity is unsold value and excludes future disposal costs. Full-term interest assumes the entered rate continues.

Inputs are bounded for stable modelling, not to indicate lender criteria. View source records and the automated engine tests.
