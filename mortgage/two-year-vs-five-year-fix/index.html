What would change the result?

The two paths have equal five-year financing costs at an assumed reset rate of approximately 4.54%. Above that reset rate, the five-year path has lower modelled costs.

Option A resets once, at month 25, to the rate you enter for the rest of the five-year comparison. Its new fee is paid in cash. Option B holds its rate for five years. This is a scenario, not a forecast.

Neither path includes an early exit during the five years. Flexibility, future eligibility and the risk of unaffordable payments are separate considerations.

How this comparison works

Both options are assessed over 60 months. The shorter fix resets once after month 24. Change the future rate to explore uncertainty instead of assuming today’s rate will still be available.

What is included

A monthly repayment mortgage schedule, interest charged on the remaining balance, the entered fees and the same comparison date for both options. Principal repayments appear in cash paid but are not treated as a financing expense.

What is not included

Daily lender interest conventions, affordability checks, changing rates unless explicitly modelled, insurance and lender-specific restrictions. Overpayment tools do not calculate penalties. Full-term figures, where shown, assume unchanged rates.

A worked example

With £200,000 initially borrowed, the starting monthly outgoings are £1,078 for two-year fix, then reset and £1,112 for five-year fix. Over 60 months, interest and fees is £45,990 and £43,415 respectively. These are the page’s default figures, not market quotes; changing the inputs above replaces the report.

A common mistake to avoid

Neither path includes an early exit during the five years. Flexibility, future eligibility and the risk of unaffordable payments are separate considerations.

Questions about this calculation

Does the lower figure mean I should choose that option?

No. It means a lower modelled cost under the inputs. Eligibility, cash reserves, flexibility and the home you need are outside the calculation.

Can the future mortgage rate be predicted?

No. Enter several possible reset rates at month 25. The equal-cost rate is a mathematical threshold, not a market forecast.
