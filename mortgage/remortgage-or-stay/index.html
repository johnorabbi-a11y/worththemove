How this comparison works

A rate reduction has to earn back its setup costs. Compare the same remaining term first, then test a different term separately.

What is included

A monthly repayment mortgage schedule, interest charged on the remaining balance, the entered fees and the same comparison date for both options. Principal repayments appear in cash paid but are not treated as a financing expense.

What is not included

Daily lender interest conventions, affordability checks, changing rates unless explicitly modelled, insurance and lender-specific restrictions. Overpayment tools do not calculate penalties. Full-term figures, where shown, assume unchanged rates.

A worked example

With £200,000 initially borrowed, the starting monthly outgoings are £1,169 for stay on current deal and £1,112 for switch mortgage. Over 60 months, interest and fees is £47,311 and £43,415 respectively. These are the page’s default figures, not market quotes; changing the inputs above replaces the report.

A common mistake to avoid

Changing the term at the same time can disguise a more expensive loan behind a smaller monthly payment.

Questions about this calculation

Does the lower figure mean I should choose that option?

No. It means a lower modelled cost under the inputs. Eligibility, cash reserves, flexibility and the home you need are outside the calculation.

Why are payments and interest different?

Payments include money that reduces your balance. Interest and fees are the cost of borrowing; principal repayment reduces a debt and is tracked separately.
